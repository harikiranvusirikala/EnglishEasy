# EnglishEasy
## Product Vision & Learning Architecture (v2, 2026-11-07)

The original vision is kept unchanged in [INITIAL_VISION.md](INITIAL_VISION.md). This version adds the owner's decisions (2026-11-07), a tighter lesson design and a revised Week 1. How it is built lives in [BUILD_PLAN.md](BUILD_PLAN.md).

### What changed from the initial vision

- **Lesson arc:** nine lesson parts become six short sections (Warm-up, Learn, Practice, Listen & Speak, Role Play, Wrap-up), each 2–3 minutes, so a lesson takes 10–15 minutes and one section fits one sitting.
- **Week 1 revised:** "am / is / are" and "right now" (present continuous) added; yes/no questions are taught inside every tense lesson instead of on a separate day. Question words move to Week 2.
- **New learning features:** colour-coded sentence patterns, Telugu → English word-order bridge, "Don't say / Say" cards for common Telugu-speaker mistakes, recognise → build → produce ordering, warm-up recall, "Practice again" queue, "My Sentences" phrasebook, "Try it today" real-life mission.
- **No typing anywhere.** Every answer is a button, a word tile or the learner's voice.
- **Speaking never blocks progress.** If the microphone or speech recognition fails, the learner chooses the answer instead.
- **Day numbers, not weekdays.** Learners go at their own pace, so "Day 3", never "Wednesday".
- **Product decisions:** optional Google sign-in (guest first), phone's built-in female Indian-English voice, installable web app, Telugu + English labels, larger text option, Day/Night theme, search-engine-ready landing and lesson pages.

# 1. Core Vision

EnglishEasy is a **structured, mobile-first English learning and speaking practice app for Telugu-speaking adults and elders**.

It combines a structured daily journey, practical grammar taught through everyday sentence patterns, Telugu explanations, many learning and practice formats, listening, speaking, role play, repetition and simple visual progress.

The learner never has to decide:

> "What should I learn today?"

EnglishEasy answers that for them — while never locking them into a rigid course.

---

# 2. Who It Is For & Design Principles

**The learner:** a Telugu-speaking parent or grandparent. Reads Telugu comfortably, knows some English words, uses WhatsApp and YouTube on an Android phone, may have weaker eyesight, may be nervous about "getting it wrong".

Principles every screen follows:

1. **One clear next step.** Every screen has one obvious primary button.
2. **No typing.** Answers are buttons, word tiles or voice. There are no text fields in the app.
3. **Telugu + English labels.** Buttons and menus show the English word with its Telugu meaning (e.g. **Continue** · కొనసాగించు). The learner always understands, and picks up everyday app English for free.
4. **Big and calm.** Large text (with A / A+ / A++ sizes), large touch targets, generous spacing, high contrast in Day and Night themes.
5. **Never blocked, never punished.** No lives, no hearts, no streak that resets, no red "wrong" screens. Mistakes say "Almost" and show the right answer with a short Telugu reason.
6. **Encouraging, not competitive.** "You practised 4 days this week", never leaderboards.
7. **Works on a basic phone, on patchy data.** Installable, fast, works offline after the first visit.

# 3. Learning Journey

```text
Course
  └── Week (a theme, e.g. "English Foundations")
       └── Day / Lesson (one focused pattern)
            └── Section (Warm-up, Learn, Practice, Listen & Speak, Role Play, Wrap-up)
                 └── Activity (one card, question or conversation)
```

> **Weeks provide structure; lessons provide focused learning; scenarios provide real-world context.**

### Flexible pacing

The app recommends **one lesson per day** and enforces nothing:

- Finished Day 3 and want Day 4 now? Allowed.
- Want to redo Day 3? Allowed.
- Want only speaking or role play practice? Allowed, from the Practice tab.
- Any lesson can be opened at any time; nothing is locked.

```text
Today's lesson
Day 3 — Every Day

You can continue to Day 4 whenever you're ready.
```

# 4. Lesson Design

Every lesson teaches **one sentence pattern** through **6–8 core sentences**, and every activity in the lesson reuses those sentences in a different format. Seeing the same sentences in many formats is what makes the pattern stick.

## 4.1 The six sections

| # | Section | Time | What happens |
|---|---------|------|--------------|
| 1 | **Warm-up** · గుర్తుచేసుకుందాం | 1 min | 2 quick questions from earlier lessons (from Day 2 on). Recall makes memory stronger. |
| 2 | **Learn** · నేర్చుకుందాం | 3 min | The pattern, the Telugu bridge, examples with audio, "When do I use this?", "Don't say / Say". |
| 3 | **Practice** · సాధన | 4 min | 6–8 questions, easy to hard: recognise → build → produce → find the mistake. |
| 4 | **Listen & Speak** · విని మాట్లాడండి | 3 min | Hear and choose, listen and repeat, answer a real-life situation by voice. |
| 5 | **Role Play** · సంభాషణ | 2 min | A short conversation in a real scene (3–5 turns). |
| 6 | **Wrap-up** · ముగింపు | 1 min | "Today you learned", key sentences saved to My Sentences, "Try it today" mission. |

Total: about **15 activities, 10–15 minutes**. A learner can stop after any section and continue exactly where they left off.

## 4.2 Teaching tools used in every lesson

**Colour-coded sentence pattern.** Every pattern is shown as coloured blocks with Telugu labels. The same colours mean the same thing in every lesson, so the learner builds a visual grammar without grammar terms:

```text
[ I ]        [ went ]         [ to the market ]   [ yesterday ]
 ఎవరు          ఏం చేశారు          ఎక్కడికి / ఏమిటి        ఎప్పుడు
 Who          Action            What / Where          When
```

Colour is never the only signal: each block also carries its label.

**Telugu → English bridge.** Telugu puts the action at the end; English puts it second. Shown with the same coloured blocks moving into place:

```text
నేను    నిన్న     మార్కెట్‌కి     వెళ్లాను
 I    yesterday   to the market    went
           ↓
I  went  to the market  yesterday.
```

**"When do I use this?"** For every form: what it means, when to use it, how to say it, and what not to say (e.g. can / could / would; every day vs right now).

**"Don't say / Say."** One or two common mistakes Telugu speakers make with the lesson's pattern, with a one-line Telugu reason:

```text
✗ Yesterday I go to the market.
✓ Yesterday I went to the market.
"నిన్న" అంటే జరిగిపోయింది — went వాడాలి.
```

**Audio everywhere.** Every English sentence has 🔊 (normal) and 🐢 (slow) buttons.

**Hints.** Build and produce questions have a **Hint** · సహాయం button (shows the first word or the pattern).

## 4.3 Mistake policy

1. Wrong answer → "Almost" · దాదాపు, the right answer, and a short Telugu reason. The learner tries once more.
2. Wrong again → show the answer, add the question to **Practice again**, move on.
3. No penalties. The lesson still completes.

## 4.4 Practice order inside a lesson

```text
Recognise       →  Build              →  Produce              →  Check
choose, listen     arrange word tiles     Telugu → English tiles   find the mistake
                   fill the blank         speak the answer
```

Difficulty also rises across the course (Section 11), but within each lesson the learner always starts with what is easiest.

# 5. Activity Types

No activity needs typing.

| Activity | The learner… | Used in |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Explain card** | reads the pattern, the Telugu bridge and examples; taps 🔊 | Learn |
| **When-to-use card** | compares forms (can / could / would) with examples | Learn |
| **Don't say / Say card** | sees a common mistake and its fix | Learn |
| **Choose** | picks the right sentence or meaning (2–4 buttons) | Warm-up, Practice |
| **Choose the response** | someone says something; picks the right reply ("Yes, please." not "Yes, I am.") | Practice |
| **Fill the blank** | taps the word that completes the sentence | Practice |
| **Arrange** | taps word tiles in order to build the sentence | Practice |
| **Translate** | sees Telugu, builds the English with word tiles (a few extra tiles as distractors) | Practice |
| **Match** | pairs Telugu meanings with English sentences | Practice |
| **Find the mistake** | taps the wrong word in a sentence, sees the fix | Practice |
| **Listen and choose** | hears a sentence (no text), picks its meaning | Listen & Speak |
| **Listen and repeat** | hears a sentence, says it aloud | Listen & Speak |
| **Situation** | reads a situation in Telugu ("You are at a shop. Ask the price."), answers by voice — or taps one of the choices | Listen & Speak |
| **Role play** | takes part in a short conversation by voice or by tapping a reply | Role Play |

Every multi-question part shows progress:

```text
Practice
Question 6 of 8
████████████░░░░
```

and ends with a short summary:

```text
Practice complete 🎉
7 / 8 correct
1 sentence added to Practice again.

[Continue]
```

# 6. Listening & Speaking

**Voice (listening).** The phone's built-in voice, preferring a clear **female Indian-English voice** (en-IN). If the phone has none, the best available female English voice. Learners can pick another voice in Settings and hear a sample. Normal speed is slightly slower than conversation; 🐢 is slower still. No audio files, no cost, works offline.

**Speech recognition (speaking).** The phone's built-in recognition (Indian English). Judging is forgiving:

- Matches the meaning of the expected words, ignoring punctuation, filler words and contractions ("I am" = "I'm").
- **Great** · చాలా బాగుంది / **Good** · బాగుంది / **Try again** · మళ్ళీ ప్రయత్నించండి — no numeric scores on single attempts.
- After two tries, **Choose instead** · ఎంచుకోండి appears, and the learner continues.

**When speaking is not possible** (no microphone permission, unsupported phone, noisy room): every speaking activity offers **Choose instead**, and a Settings switch "I can't speak now" turns speaking activities into choosing activities for the session. Speaking never blocks lesson completion.

# 7. Role Play

Role play makes the learner feel they are really in a scene.

```text
Restaurant — You are the customer.

🧑‍🍳 Waiter: Good evening. What would you like?   🔊
                 (శుభ సాయంత్రం. మీకు ఏమి కావాలి?)

🎤 Speak your reply      or tap:
  [ I would like a dosa and coffee. ]
  [ I am a dosa. ]
  [ Yes, please. ]
```

- Conversations are **predefined turns with acceptable replies** — no AI at runtime.
- The partner's line is spoken aloud; its Telugu meaning is one tap away.
- A Hint shows the start of a good reply.
- V1 conversations are linear (3–5 turns); a wrong reply gets a gentle correction and the conversation continues.

**Role-play summary:**

```text
Role play complete 🎉

Good:
✓ Greeting
✓ Ordering food

Practice more:
• "Would you like…?"

Speaking: 8 / 10      Conversation: 7 / 10
```

Scores are simple (out of 10): speaking = how well spoken replies matched, conversation = right replies chosen or spoken.

# 8. Review & Repetition

| Tool | How it works |
|------|--------------|
| **Warm-up** | Each lesson (from Day 2) starts with 2 questions from earlier lessons. |
| **Practice again** | Questions answered wrong twice are kept here; one correct answer removes them. |
| **Weekly review (Day 7)** | Mixes all the week's patterns in new combinations, plus a longer role play. |
| **My Sentences** | A phrasebook of each finished lesson's key sentences, with 🔊 and 🎤 — quick speaking practice and a handy reference ("how do I say this to the doctor?"). |
| **Try it today** | Each lesson ends with one real-life mission: "Today, tell someone in your family what you did yesterday — in English." |

# 9. Progress & Completion

## 9.1 What the learner always sees

Progress answers three questions immediately:

- **Where am I?** — Week 1, Day 4
- **How much is done?** — 70%
- **What remains?** — 5 activities remaining

```text
Day 4 — Right Now                75%
████████████░░░░
12 of 16 done · 4 left

✓ Warm-up
✓ Learn
✓ Practice
● Listen & Speak
○ Role Play
○ Wrap-up
```

## 9.2 Completion rule

- An activity is **done** when it was answered (right or wrong), a card was viewed to the end, a speaking activity was attempted (spoken or "Choose instead"), or a role play was finished.
- **Lesson %** = done activities ÷ all activities in the lesson.
- A **lesson is complete** when every activity in its required sections is done. Each lesson's content says which sections are required (by default all; a lesson may mark a section optional). The rule lives in content, not code.
- Opening a lesson never completes it.

## 9.3 What is tracked (hidden from the learner)

Per activity: done, correct, attempts, last time. Plus the Practice-again queue, the days the learner practised, and the exact resume position. Lesson, section, week and course progress are calculated from this; states shown are **Not started ○ / In progress ● / Complete ✓**.

## 9.4 Continue experience

```text
Welcome back 👋
You were learning Past Tense — Yesterday.
6 of 15 done · 9 left

[Continue]
```

Continue returns to the exact activity the learner left.

# 11. Curriculum

## 11.1 Week 1 — English Foundations (V1)

Each tense lesson teaches the **three forms together**: positive, negative and yes/no question with short answers. Time words anchor each tense on a simple timeline: **every day · right now · yesterday · tomorrow**. Tense names appear only as a small subtitle.

| Day | Title | Pattern | Core sentences (examples) | Don't say → Say | Role play | Try it today |
|-----|-------|---------|---------------------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------|
| 1 | **Who Does What** · వాక్య నిర్మాణం *(sentence basics)* | Who + Action + What; Telugu verb-last vs English verb-second | I eat rice. I drink tea. I read the newspaper. We speak Telugu. They play cricket. | I rice eat. → I eat rice. | Grandchild asks what you drink in the morning | Say three things you do, in English |
| 2 | **I Am, You Are** · నేను, నువ్వు *(pronouns + am/is/are)* | Who + am/is/are + what/how | I am Lakshmi. I am hungry. He is my son. She is a teacher. We are happy. Are you tired? Yes, I am. | I hungry. → I am hungry. | Meeting a new neighbour | Introduce yourself to a family member |
| 3 | **Every Day** · రోజూ *(present simple)* | I walk / She walks; don't / doesn't; Do / Does …? | I walk every morning. She goes to the temple on Fridays. He doesn't drink coffee. Do you watch the news? Yes, I do. | He go to office. → He goes to the office. | Doctor asks about your daily routine | Tell someone your morning routine |
| 4 | **Right Now** · ఇప్పుడు *(present continuous)* | am/is/are + verb-ing | I am cooking. She is sleeping. They are watching TV. I am not working now. Are you coming? Yes, I am. | Now I cook. → I am cooking now. I am knowing him. → I know him. | Son calls: "What are you doing?" | Say what you are doing right now |
| 5 | **Yesterday** · నిన్న *(past simple)* | went / ate / bought; didn't + verb; Did …? | I went to the market yesterday. I bought vegetables. We watched a film. I didn't go out. Did you eat? Yes, I did. | Yesterday I go. → Yesterday I went. I didn't went. → I didn't go. | Friend asks what you did yesterday | Tell someone what you did yesterday |
| 6 | **Tomorrow** · రేపు *(future with will)* | will + verb; won't; Will …? | I will call you tomorrow. We will go to the temple. She will come on Sunday. I won't be late. Will you help me? Yes, I will. | I will going. → I will go. | Planning a visit to a relative's house | Tell someone one plan for tomorrow |
| 7 | **My Day** · నా రోజు *(weekly review)* | One verb across the timeline: I go / I am going / I went / I will go | Mixed from Days 1–6 | Mixed | Video call with grandchild: talk about your day — every day, now, yesterday, tomorrow | Talk about your day to someone for one minute |

Day 7 has a **timeline activity**: the same action placed on every day / right now / yesterday / tomorrow, then mixed review questions across the week, then the longest role play so far, then the Week complete screen.

| Week | Theme | Lessons |

## 11.3 Concepts to cover across the course

Taught when needed for communication, never as grammar for its own sake:

- **Pronouns:** I / you / he / she / it / we / they; my / your / his / her / our / their
- **Be:** am / is / are / was / were
- **Do:** do / does / did · **Have:** have / has / had
- **Modals:** can / could / should / would / will (each with a "When do I use this?" card)
- **Articles:** a / an / the · **Prepositions:** in / on / at / to / from / for / with
- **Connectors:** and / but / because / so / if
- **Question words:** what / where / when / why / who / how
- **Quantities:** some / any / much / many / more / less · **Modifiers:** very / too / enough / also / only

**"How do I say this?" expressions**, grouped by intention: suggesting, asking, offering, conveying information, clarifying, agreeing, disagreeing politely. The learner learns **which expression fits which situation** (see INITIAL_VISION.md §12 for examples).

## 11.4 Grammar meets real life

Every concept travels the same path, and reappears in later weeks in new scenes (past tense in shopping, travel, the doctor's visit…):

**Pattern → Sentence → Situation → Conversation**

```text
Past tense → "I went to the market yesterday." → "What did you buy?" → Role play: tell a friend about yesterday
```

# 13. Accounts & Data

- **Guest first.** Anyone can start Day 1 immediately. Progress is saved on the phone.
- **Optional Google sign-in** ("Save your progress with Google"), offered after the first lesson and in Settings. On sign-in, the phone's progress is merged into the account (nothing is lost on either side), and from then on progress is kept in sync across devices.
- Stored per account: learning progress only (activities done, Practice-again queue, practice days, resume position) plus the Google name, email and photo for the greeting and Settings. Theme, text size and voice stay on the device.
- A plain-language privacy page explains this (also required by Google for sign-in).

# 14. Look & Feel

- **Production quality:** consistent spacing, one set of reusable components, smooth short animations, skeletons while loading, no layout jumps.
- **Themes:** Day, Night, Follow phone — no flash of the wrong theme on load.
- **Text:** large base size; A / A+ / A++ setting; Telugu set in Noto Sans Telugu with comfortable line spacing.
- **Colours:** calm and warm. Correct = green with ✓; try again = amber with ↻ (never red for a learner's answer); the pattern colours are the same in every lesson; colour is never the only signal.
- **Delight, lightly:** a confetti burst on lesson and week completion, friendly messages, no noise.
- **Accessible:** large touch targets, visible focus, screen-reader names, respects reduced motion.

---

# 15. Reach

- **Search engines:** a static landing page (what it is, how it works, a lesson preview, FAQ) and indexable lesson overview pages ("Past tense in English — explained in Telugu"), with structured data, sitemap and robots for production only, and Google Search Console verification.
- **WhatsApp sharing:** a good link preview image and the Week-complete "Share with family" button.
- **Installable app:** "Add to Home screen" gives an app icon, full-screen app and offline lessons. Home shows a one-time "Install EnglishEasy" card with the right instructions for Android or iPhone.
- **Analytics:** Google Tag Manager on the landing page only, never inside lessons.
- **Support:** a "Buy me a coffee" link in the landing footer and Settings.

---

# 16. Not in V1

Runtime AI, paid audio voices, sound effects, leaderboards and streak penalties, typing answers, branching role plays, levels or placement tests, Telugu voice for explanations, a native Play Store app, more than Week 1 of content.

---

# 17. Product North Star

The measure of EnglishEasy is not how many grammar topics the learner completed. It is:

> **Can the learner understand and confidently use English in more everyday situations than before?**

The first 7-day journey succeeds when a Telugu-speaking parent thinks:

> **"I understand this. I can say this. I want to practise again tomorrow."**

Every feature should support that goal.
